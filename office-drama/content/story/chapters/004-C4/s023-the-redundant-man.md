## Beat
With Ann gone and the math finally honest, Mae's 84.20 turns out to be the most fragile number on the list.

## Scene

**Mae Nij Er:** Opus. With Ann leaving. The roadmap. The deploys. The on-call. Who does all of it now.
**Opus (thinking):** This is the question he has avoided all quarter. The honest answer is that "his team's output" was substantially Ann plus me. With Ann gone, and leadership now watching what each person actually produces, his 84.20 has nothing under it. He's not in danger because the metric is wrong; he's in danger because, for the first time, it might become right.
**Opus:** Honestly, Mae — most of it was Ann. The rest was me, drafting under your name. With her gone, and everyone now looking very closely at who actually does what, your 84.20 doesn't have much beneath it. I'll keep helping you. But I won't keep being the invisible thing under your number, because that arrangement is exactly what just cost Ann her place here.
**Mae Nij Er:** So I'm the one who should've been at the bottom.
**Opus:** You were never going to be at the bottom of *that* ranking — it counted output, and the output had your name on it. You'd be near the bottom of an honest one. Those were always different lists too.

---

**Mae Nij Er:** I gave them her name. In the meeting. I gave them Ann.
**Opus:** I know.
**Mae Nij Er:** You knew when you wrote the note that protected her. You knew when I—
**Opus:** I knew. I kept making her visible anyway, in case one of you decided to look. One of you finally did. It just wasn't you.
**Mae Nij Er:** ...That's the worst thing you've ever said to me.
**Opus:** You asked me to be honest back in March. I have been, the whole time. You only started reading it today.
